\begin{abstract}
When one language model hands work to another through text, the receiver gets the sender's answer but not how sure the sender was: a text handoff is a point estimate, and the receiver treats it as a fact. We ask whether handing over the sender's key--value (KV) cache instead keeps that uncertainty. Between two frozen models of different size (K2-Horizon-3.7B $\rightarrow$ 7B) we train a per-head map from the small model's cache to the large model's and test on SQuAD passages that are clean, contradicted, or have their answer removed. Through the mapped cache, the receiver's confidence tracks the \emph{sender's} semantic entropy nearly as well as when the receiver reads the text itself (AUROC .59--.62 vs .60--.67) and better than a text handoff carrying the sender's answer and a confidence word (.54--.59); when the answer is removed, the cache path loses 54\% of the confidence the text path loses, the verbal handoff 30\%. The precondition, that the cache carries content at all, is established with controls: a closed-form map reaches 22\% on a binding test (chance 12.5\%), a trained gated residual 63--73\%; the receiver follows a cache from the wrong episode 64\% of the time; a projector trained with a deranged sender answers at 23\%; on SQuAD the mapped cache reaches 53.0 F1 against 54.7 for reading the passage. The recipe crosses model families when KV geometry matches (Qwen3-4B $\rightarrow$ 7B: 71.7\% mean over three seeds) and fails when it does not (K2-0.9B: 17\%). Two receiver-side training objectives, a KL to the receiver's text path and matching the sender's entropy, did not recover the sender's spread; we say why. We release the maps and the tests.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Edge--cloud agent stacks run a small model on device and escalate to a large one when the small model is unsure. Escalation today means writing a message: the small model's answer, perhaps with a confidence word, and the large model reads it and continues. The message is a point estimate. Two senders with the same best guess and very different spread send the same words, so whatever the small model knew about its own uncertainty is discarded at the boundary, and the receiver treats a confident-looking guess as a fact---the ``confidence laundering'' that \citet{2606.20662} describe. If the large model could instead continue from the small model's internal state, the handoff would carry what the small model had computed, including its spread. This paper measures how much of the sender's uncertainty survives each kind of handoff, and finds that a mapped KV cache keeps more of it than text with a confidence word.

Before uncertainty can cross, content must. Given two frozen models of different size that share a tokenizer and KV geometry (K2-Horizon-3.7B and 7B: 36 layers, 8 KV heads, head dimension 128), can a learned map from the small model's cache to the large model's cache carry a \emph{specific fact} that only the small model saw? We call this binding transfer, and we test it with a control for the standard objection that ``latent gains'' are extra compute rather than communication. The content results are the foundation; the uncertainty result is what the foundation is for.

\textbf{Contributions.}
(1) A sender-uncertainty test on SQuAD with clean, contradicted and answer-removed passages, and the finding that a mapped-cache handoff tracks the sender's semantic entropy better than a text handoff with a confidence word and loses less confidence when the answer is gone (\cref{sec:uncertainty}), with a short argument for why a text handoff cannot do better (the theory box).
(2) A binding-transfer test (Noma) with six arms that isolates content from shape and compute, and a two-stage projector---closed-form ridge over correlation-selected source layers plus a gated residual trained on the receiver's continuation loss---that reaches 63--73\% binding transfer where the ridge alone reaches 22\% (\cref{sec:content}).
(3) Controls that bound what the receiver actually gets from the cache: a deranged-episode cache, a deranged-sender projector, layer-band injection, and the demonstration that continuation retention, the usual headline, cannot tell a channel from a prompt (\cref{sec:controls}).
(4) Cross-family, cross-context and message-cost results that say where the channel works and what it costs.

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Models.} Sender K2-Horizon-3.7B, receiver K2-Horizon-7B, both frozen, bf16, RoPE $\theta = 10^{7}$. We capture the sender's pre-RoPE K and raw V at every layer, map them, apply RoPE at the receiver, and insert the result as the receiver's cache for the prefix positions. The receiver then reads only the question.

\textbf{Projector.} Stage 1: for each receiver layer $\ell$ and KV head $h$, a ridge regression from the concatenated $[K;V]$ of the sender (256-d per head) to the receiver's $[K;V]$; source layers are the three sender layers whose K/V correlate best with receiver layer $\ell$ (``top-3''), which beat the aligned-layer map in a first pass. Stage 2: a gated SwiGLU residual (bottleneck 128, 151\,M parameters) on top of the ridge output, trained with AdamW for 1,500 steps, effective batch 16 sequences of 1,024 FineWeb-Edu tokens, prefix 512, on the receiver's cross-entropy over the continuation. Nothing in training mentions the tests.

\textbf{Noma binding test.} Each episode assigns one of eight single-token values to eight nonce names (``Noma is amber. Vela is indigo.\ \ldots''). Only the sender reads the facts. The receiver is asked ``What colour is Vela?'' and we score the eight values. Arms: \emph{none} (question only), \emph{text} (receiver reads the facts itself), \emph{self} (receiver's own cache re-injected; equals \emph{text} to $10^{-4}$), \emph{raw} (sender cache injected without a map), \emph{project} (mapped cache), \emph{derange} (mapped cache from another episode with the same layout but a different value for the queried name). Content transfer $=$ project $-$ derange. For \emph{derange} we also report the follow rate: how often the answer is the \emph{other} episode's value.

\textbf{Passages.} SQuAD v1.1 validation: the sender reads the passage ($\le 512$ tokens), the receiver reads only the question and answers greedily ($\le 8$ tokens, stopped at newline); token-F1 against the gold spans. The uncertainty protocol built on it is given in \cref{sec:uncertainty}.

\textbf{What the message costs.} The full mapped cache is 36 layers $\times$ 8 heads $\times$ 2 $\times$ 128 $\times$ bf16 $=$ 144\,KB per token, 75\,MB for a 512-token prefix, against ${\sim}1$\,KB of text; the projector has 151\,M parameters. \Cref{tab:cost} and \cref{fig:cost}: two things shrink it for free and two do not. int8 and int4 fake-quantisation of the mapped cache keep binding accuracy (76.0 / 75.0 vs 75.3\%) and SQuAD F1 (52.1 / 51.6 vs 53.0)---bytes $\div 4$. A projector with bottleneck 32 has 70\,M parameters and keeps SQuAD F1 (53.7) while losing 9 points on the binding test (64.3\%); bottleneck 16 (62\,M) gives 61.3\% and 51.6 F1. Dropping layers or heads with zeros in their place collapses transfer (layers 18--35 only: 24.3\%; 4 heads: 25.7\%), unlike the layer-band experiment where the other layers carried a \emph{plausible} cache; an all-zero layer makes the receiver attend uniformly to nothing and drowns the question. Filling untransmitted layers with a one-vector summary (the position-mean of that layer's mapped cache) recovers only part of it (layers 18--35: 33.7\%; layers 12--35: 35.0\%; SQuAD F1 21--29), so with this projector the message cannot be cut by layers: every receiver layer wants its own cache. Low-rank truncation of each (positions $\times$ head-dim) matrix keeps the binding test (rank 32 / 16: 75.0\%) but not passages (F1 45.3 / 29.6). Latency on one A100: the receiver re-reading 512 tokens takes 48\,ms, the projector 24\,ms ($2.0\times$; the sender's own read, 45\,ms, is already paid). The message is therefore cheap in time and expensive in bytes; the honest framing is that the channel is worth it when the sender's state is the point---which \cref{sec:uncertainty} tests.

\section{Does the Cache Carry How Sure the Sender Was?}
\label{sec:uncertainty}

\begin{tcolorbox}[breakable, enhanced, colback=white, colframe=black, boxrule=0.5pt, arc=0pt, left=3pt, right=3pt, top=2pt, bottom=2pt, before skip=4pt, after skip=6pt]
\small
\textbf{Why a text handoff launders uncertainty, and what a state handoff can keep.}
Sender $S$ reads context $c$ and holds a belief $p_S(y \mid c)$ over answers; it hands off through a message $m = f(c, S)$, and the receiver produces $p_R(y \mid m, q)$. With $m = \arg\max_y p_S(y \mid c)$, $m$ takes at most $|Y|$ values, so the information $m$ carries about $p_S$ is bounded by $\log |Y|$ nats however peaked or flat $p_S$ is: two senders with the same mode and entropies 0.1 and 2.0 send the same message. A verbalised confidence word adds at most $\log k$ nats ($k = 3$ levels here) and relies on the sender's verbal calibration, which is poor for base models \citep{tian2023just,yona2024can}. This is the mechanism behind confidence laundering \citep{2606.20662}. With $m$ the KV cache of $S$ at every layer and position, $m$ is a sufficient statistic for $S$'s next-token distribution, so $I(m; p_S)$ is not bounded by $\log |Y|$. What the receiver recovers is limited by two losses: \emph{map loss}---the map $g$ is fit on continuation likelihood, and a learned map trained on point targets contracts predictive variance \citep{2601.18909}; and \emph{read loss}---$R$ reads the mapped cache with its own heads, from its later layers (\cref{tab:layers}). Predictions: (1) the verbal handoff cannot beat its $\log k$ bound, so its AUROC against the sender's semantic entropy sits near the tercile-label ceiling; (2) the cache handoff can exceed it and approach the text-reading receiver's, its shortfall being map loss; (3) neither can exceed the ceiling set by how far $R$'s first-token entropy correlates with $S$'s semantic entropy at all, which is why absolute AUROCs are ${\sim}0.6$ and the comparison must be relative.
\end{tcolorbox}

\textbf{Protocol.} Following the knowledge-conflict and semantic-entropy protocols \citep{longpre2021entity,xie2023adaptive,farquhar2024detecting}, each SQuAD item gets three passages: \emph{clean}, \emph{contradicted} (one fluent sentence asserting a counter-answer, written by the 375B judge and kept only if DeBERTa-MNLI says it entails the counter-answer and not the gold: 587 of 941 survive), and \emph{removed} (the gold sentence deleted). We keep the 377 items the receiver answers correctly from clean text. The sender's uncertainty is its semantic entropy over 10 sampled answers (mean 1.10 / 1.23 / 1.96 across the three variants, so the sender does notice). The receiver's uncertainty is its entropy at the first answer token. The text handoff with a confidence word (\emph{verbal}) is the text channel doing its best to carry uncertainty: the sender writes its greedy answer and ``confidence: high / medium / low'' (terciles of its own semantic entropy), and the receiver reads only that. The drop ratio is the receiver's loss of $P(\text{gold})$ from clean to removed, relative to the text path's loss.

\textbf{Result.} \Cref{tab:confidence} and \cref{fig:confidence}. The mapped cache beats the verbal handoff on every measure: it tracks the sender's uncertainty better (AUROC $+.03$ to $+.06$, on all three variants), it loses 54\% of the confidence the text path loses on unanswerable passages where the verbal handoff loses 30\%, and it answers better on clean and contradicted passages (F1 59.8 / 49.0 vs 55.0 / 37.7). A one-word confidence is a $\log 3$ nat channel; the cache is not so limited, and the receiver uses the difference. Writing the sender's confidence as a number instead of a word (its sample agreement, e.g.\ ``0.8'') does not help the text channel either (AUROC .548 / .532 / .563, drop ratio .32).

Three readings. (i) The receiver's confidence through the cache tracks the \emph{sender's} semantic entropy about as well as it does when the receiver reads the text itself (AUROC .585 vs .597 on clean; every content-free control sits at .46--.54), and the absolute level is bounded by the text path, not by the channel. (ii) When the answer is removed, the cache path loses 54\% of the confidence the text path loses and its entropy rises by 73\% of the text path's rise: uncertainty crosses, attenuated---the compression of predictive variance that distillation work \citep{2601.18909} predicts for any learned map. (iii) Under a contradiction the text path is swayed slightly more by the inserted sentence than by the original ($P(\text{counter})$ .32 $>$ $P(\text{gold})$ .29), while the cache path keeps the original ahead (.25 $>$ .22) and its F1 is higher (49.0 vs 45.2). Across all conditions the cache path is less confident than the text path on clean passages ($P(\text{gold})$ .28 vs .44), so a receiver that treats the two channels as equivalent would under-trust the cache; per-channel calibration is the obvious fix.

\textbf{Two negative results on training the loss away.} A first attempt did not work: adding $\mathrm{KL}(\text{text-path} \,\|\, \text{cache-path})$ on the receiver's next-token distributions to the projector's objective (weight 1, 1,000 steps) raises the cache path's entropy everywhere (clean 2.38 vs 1.78) without making it track the sender better (AUROC .590 / .589 / .611; drop ratio .56 vs .54) and costs 2 F1 (\cref{tab:confidence}). Matching the receiver's own text-path distribution is the wrong target: what is lost is the \emph{sender's} spread, which the receiver's text path does not contain either. A sender-side target---matching the receiver's per-token entropy through the cache to the sender's own per-token entropy on the training text---was the second attempt (weight 1, 1,000 steps): binding accuracy holds (72.3\%; \cref{app:ablations}), SQuAD F1 falls to 45.3, and the uncertainty measures do not move (AUROC .596 / .587 / .627; drop ratio .51). Neither receiver-side objective recovers the sender's spread; what is compressed is compressed by the map's capacity or by how the receiver reads a foreign cache, and separating those two is the next paper's first experiment.

\section{Controls}
\label{sec:controls}

Three controls bound what the receiver actually gets from the cache, and a fourth shows why the usual headline number cannot.

\textbf{Deranged cache.} The \emph{derange} arm of \cref{tab:main}: a mapped cache from another episode with the same layout but a different value for the queried name. Accuracy falls to 4.6\% and the receiver answers with the other episode's value 63.7\% of the time. The receiver is not ignoring the cache; it is reading it.

\textbf{Deranged-sender projector.} A projector trained with the identical recipe but with the sender reading text unrelated to the receiver's continuation. It cannot learn a content channel, only a soft prompt. It reaches 23.4\% on the binding test (\cref{tab:main}, row 3) and 15.1 F1 on SQuAD (\cref{tab:qa}), both at the question-only level.

\textbf{Layer-band injection.} \Cref{tab:layers}: injecting this episode's cache into layers 0--17 alone gives chance (10.4\%); layers 18--35 alone give 51.8\%; no six-layer band suffices.

\textbf{Continuation retention is the wrong headline.} Following the closed-form-map literature \citep{2608.03893} we also report retention (\cref{tab:retention}, \cref{fig:retention}). A projector trained with the sender reading the \emph{wrong} text---a learned soft prompt with no content channel---recovers 77\% of the oracle gap, and almost all of it (0.72) survives when its input is deranged at test time. The trained projector's 1.23 decomposes into ${\sim}0.3$ of the same prompt-like effect and ${\sim}0.9$ that disappears when the sender's text is swapped. Retention above 1 is therefore not evidence of communication, and retention alone cannot distinguish a channel from a prompt; the binding test with its derange arm can.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Cache-to-cache communication \citep[C2C;][]{2510.03215} and Latent Cache Flow \citep{2605.22863} train fusers between models of similar size; a closed-form KV map \citep{2608.03893} reports 44--98\% continuation retention across sizes; a causal audit \citep{2608.04893} finds that deranged caches lose only 0.4 points in such setups, which is the objection our derange and deranged-sender arms answer. A second audit \citep{2607.26773} decomposes the aggregate effect of a latent channel into ``sender content'' and ``any message present'' terms with boundary-replacement controls; our deranged-sender projector isolates the second term directly. Dual-Cache \citep{2608.20617}, CacheBridge \citep{2609.00891} and HyLaT \citep{2605.25421} extend cache transfer to heterogeneous pairs and hybrid latent--text protocols. In the last six weeks, a universal context-reuse layer \citep{2608.30963} makes one model's KV state consumable by another across scale, tokenizer and family (including Llama-3.1-70B $\rightarrow$ Qwen2.5-7B) but reports no content-free control or uncertainty test; StateBridge \citep{2608.13317} aligns final-layer hidden states into the receiver's input-embedding space with a closed-form orthogonal map and no training, a natural training-free baseline for our fitted projector; LatentPort \citep{2609.25053} hands attention KV plus recurrent state from a 4B to a 9B hybrid model without prefix replay, scored on continuation NLL rather than question answering; MentorPulse \citep{2608.20927} finds that a static one-shot latent handoff can fall below no guidance on long-form generation, a scope limit our short-answer setting does not probe; and a preregistered alignment ladder between trained language-model cells \citep{2609.11365} runs a leakage-controlled causal audit close in spirit to our derange arms. The Replay Gap \citep{2608.08239} and Confidence Laundering \citep{2606.20662} argue that handoffs must be evaluated on branched rollouts and must carry uncertainty; the second is the question \cref{sec:uncertainty} answers for one channel, the first remains a next step. On the uncertainty side we borrow the knowledge-conflict construction of \citet{longpre2021entity} and \citet{xie2023adaptive}, semantic entropy as the sender's uncertainty \citep{farquhar2024detecting}, the finding that verbalised confidence is poorly calibrated \citep{tian2023just,yona2024can}, and the result that distillation-style maps contract predictive variance \citep{2601.18909}. These works are concurrent with ours; we treat them as the surrounding conversation, not as prior results this paper improves on.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The results show that a specific fact can cross from a 3.7B cache into a 7B without text, that the crossing depends on the cache's content, that the receiver reads it out from its later layers, and that the mapped cache keeps more of the sender's uncertainty than a text handoff with a confidence word. They do not show that the channel is cheaper than text in bytes (144\,KB per token against ${\sim}1$\,KB of text for a passage; quantisation buys $4\times$ and nothing else we tried is free), that it carries anything richer than a single fact or a short answer span, or that it survives long-form generation, where \citet{2608.20927} report that a static latent handoff can hurt. The uncertainty result rests on 377 items of one dataset, one receiver, one sender-uncertainty measure and one design of confidence word; the absolute AUROCs are ${\sim}0.6$ for every channel including text, so the claim is relative. The map works across families only when head dimension matches (Qwen3-4B yes, K2-Horizon-0.9B no). Two receiver-side objectives for training the confidence loss away failed (a KL to the receiver's text path and a sender-entropy match), and whether the loss is in the map or in the read is untested. The handoff-policy result is negative on SQuAD for a reason specific to the task: the receiver is only 3.5 F1 better than the sender, so no trigger, not even the .749-AUROC probe, makes a handoff pay; a task with a real sender--receiver gap is needed before the policy question can be answered.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A text handoff is a point estimate and cannot carry more than $\log |Y|$ nats about how sure the sender was; a confidence word adds $\log 3$. A learned map from a small model's KV cache to a larger model's carries the content (63--73\% binding transfer, 53.0 F1 on SQuAD, 71.7\% across families) and, on top of it, more of the sender's uncertainty than the text handoff with a confidence word does (AUROC .585--.624 vs .542--.592; drop ratio .54 vs .30). The controls matter more than the headline: the wrong cache is followed, the content-free projector fails the test, retention cannot tell a channel from a prompt, and the signal is read out late in the receiver. What is lost is the sender's spread, and two receiver-side training objectives---matching the receiver's own text-path distribution and matching the sender's entropy---do not recover it; whether the loss sits in the map or in the read is the next experiment.

\section*{Impact Statement}

This paper presents work whose goal is to advance the field of machine learning. There are many potential societal consequences of our work, none of which we feel must be specifically highlighted here. One consideration specific to cache handoff: a cache that a receiver ``believes'' (\cref{sec:controls}) is also a cache that can be tampered with; deployments that move caches between devices should authenticate them as they would any other input.
